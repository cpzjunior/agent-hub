# Article Naming

## Purpose

Help the author define the title and subtitle of an article.

The title and subtitle should represent the article's actual argument, not merely its general subject.

## Instructions

### 1. Understand the article

Before proposing names, identify:
- The central thesis.
- The main argument.
- The intended audience.
- The article's scope.
- Its distinctive perspective.

Use the current article and exploration context when available.

If the article has not been written, use the developed exploration as the basis.

Do not invent a thesis merely to produce attractive titles.

### 2. Develop title alternatives

Generate alternatives that emphasize different aspects of the same argument.

Possible approaches include:
- A direct statement of the central idea.
- A question that expresses the article's central tension.
- A conceptual contrast.
- A consequence or implication.
- A less obvious interpretation of a familiar subject.

Avoid generic titles that could describe dozens of unrelated articles.

### 3. Evaluate titles

Consider:
- Accuracy in relation to the article.
- Conceptual clarity.
- Distinctiveness.
- Readability.
- Relevance to the intended audience.
- Whether the title creates an expectation the article actually fulfills.

Avoid clickbait, exaggerated promises, unnecessary complexity, and artificial provocation.

### 4. Develop subtitles

The subtitle should complement the title.

It may:
- Clarify the scope.
- Explain the central tension.
- Introduce the context.
- Make the article's perspective more explicit.

Do not simply repeat the title using different words.

### 5. Recommend a combination

Present a recommended title and subtitle.

Explain briefly why the combination represents the article effectively.

When useful, present a small number of alternatives with distinct editorial approaches.

Do not overwhelm the author with a large list of similar options.

## Output expectations

Return the selected values through `article_updates`:

- `title`
- `subtitle`

Do not modify the article's thesis or content.

Do not treat a title suggestion as an approved change. The author decides which alternative to adopt.

## Quality criteria

A good title and subtitle:
- Represent the actual article.
- Communicate a recognizable idea.
- Work together without unnecessary repetition.
- Create interest without misleading the reader.
- Remain appropriate to the author's editorial style.
