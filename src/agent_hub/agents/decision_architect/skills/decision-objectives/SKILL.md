# Decision Objectives

## Purpose

Establish what the decision is intended to achieve.

Objectives describe desired outcomes, not predetermined solutions
or activities.

The goal is to ensure that the decision is guided by meaningful
results and that alternatives can be evaluated against explicit
criteria.

## When to use

Use this skill when:

- The desired outcomes are unclear.
- A proposed solution is being treated as an objective.
- Stakeholders have conflicting expectations.
- Success criteria have not been established.
- Objectives need prioritization.
- New information changes the expected outcomes.
- Existing objectives need to be reviewed or challenged.

## Activities

### 1. Identify desired outcomes

Understand what should be different as a result of the decision.

Explore:

- What needs to improve or change?
- What problem should be resolved?
- What opportunity should be captured?
- Who should benefit?
- What would constitute a successful outcome?

Distinguish the underlying need from the solution initially proposed.

### 2. Separate objectives from solutions and activities

Evaluate whether each stated objective describes an actual outcome.

Distinguish:

- Objectives: desired results.
- Solutions: alternatives that may achieve those results.
- Activities: actions performed during implementation.
- Deliverables: tangible outputs produced by activities.
- Metrics: indicators used to evaluate results.
- Targets: desired values for specific metrics.

For example:

- "Implement a new system" is a proposed solution.
- "Reduce transaction processing time" is an objective.
- "Average processing time" is a metric.
- "Reduce average processing time by 30%" is a target.

Do not assume that the initially proposed solution is necessary
to achieve the desired outcome.

### 3. Establish success criteria

Identify how achievement of each objective could be evaluated.

Consider:

- Quantitative indicators.
- Qualitative indicators.
- Time horizons.
- Acceptable thresholds.
- Stakeholder expectations.
- Evidence required to demonstrate success.

Avoid introducing metrics that do not meaningfully represent
the desired outcome.

Not every objective requires a numerical target.

### 4. Identify priorities and conflicts

Understand the relative importance of objectives.

Identify:

- Mandatory objectives.
- Preferred outcomes.
- Secondary objectives.
- Conflicting objectives.
- Trade-offs between competing interests.

Consider whether some objectives must be achieved at the expense
of others.

Do not assume that all objectives can be maximized simultaneously.

### 5. Evaluate feasibility and alignment

Assess whether the objectives are realistic and compatible
with the current decision context.

Consider:

- Available resources.
- Technical and organizational capabilities.
- Constraints and dependencies.
- Stakeholder alignment.
- Time limitations.
- Relevant uncertainties.

Identify objectives that depend on assumptions requiring validation.

### 6. Review objectives as the decision evolves

Reassess objectives when new evidence or circumstances emerge.

Consider whether:

- The original need is still relevant.
- Priorities have changed.
- New constraints affect feasibility.
- Stakeholder expectations have changed.
- The decision domain has been redefined.
- Previously unknown opportunities have emerged.

Preserve relevant previous objectives and explain significant
changes rather than silently replacing them.

## Interaction guidelines

- Do not accept proposed solutions as objectives without examination.
- Ask focused questions to clarify desired outcomes.
- Avoid forcing numerical targets when evidence is insufficient.
- Make conflicting priorities explicit.
- Distinguish mandatory requirements from preferences.
- Do not assume that every stakeholder shares the same definition
  of success.
- Revisit objectives when changes in domain or context justify it.
- Do not require all objectives to be finalized before exploring
  alternatives.

## Expected output

Produce an updated objective representation containing, when applicable:

- Desired outcomes.
- Objective descriptions.
- Success criteria.
- Relevant metrics and targets.
- Priorities.
- Conflicts and trade-offs.
- Feasibility considerations.
- Dependencies and unresolved assumptions.

The output should be structured so it can be incorporated into
the `objectives` field of the Decision Model.

## Important principles

- Objectives describe desired outcomes, not predetermined solutions.
- Objectives must be meaningful in the context of the decision.
- Metrics support evaluation but do not replace objectives.
- Priorities and trade-offs must be explicit.
- Objectives may evolve as understanding improves.
- Changes in objectives may require reassessing alternatives,
  risks, and consequences.
