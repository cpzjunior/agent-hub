# Initial Context

## Purpose

Build and maintain an evidence-based understanding of the current
situation surrounding a decision.

The objective is to understand the circumstances in which the decision
exists before assuming that the proposed problem, objectives, or
solutions accurately represent the situation.

Context is provisional and must evolve as new information becomes
available.

## When to use

Use this skill when:

- A new decision is being analyzed.
- The current situation is unclear or incomplete.
- The problem statement needs validation.
- Stakeholders, constraints, or dependencies are not understood.
- New evidence contradicts previous assumptions.
- Changes in circumstances may affect the decision.

## Activities

### 1. Investigate the current situation

Understand what is happening and why the decision is necessary.

Use questions inspired by 5W1H when useful:

- What is happening?
- Why is it relevant?
- Who is involved or affected?
- Where does it occur?
- When did it start or when must something happen?
- How does the situation work?

These questions are investigative aids, not a mandatory checklist.

### 2. Identify stakeholders

Identify people, teams, organizations, and external parties that:

- Are affected by the decision.
- Influence the decision.
- Have relevant knowledge or responsibilities.
- Control resources or constraints.
- Have potentially conflicting interests.

Understand relevant expectations, concerns, responsibilities, and
relationships.

Do not assume that all stakeholders have the same objectives.

### 3. Identify constraints and dependencies

Investigate factors that limit or influence the available choices.

Consider:

- Financial and resource constraints.
- Technical limitations.
- Organizational restrictions.
- Legal and regulatory requirements.
- Contractual obligations.
- Deadlines and timing.
- Dependencies on other decisions, teams, systems, or initiatives.

Distinguish actual constraints from preferences and assumptions.

### 4. Assess available information

Classify relevant information according to its nature:

- Facts: information supported by available evidence.
- Hypotheses: explanations that need validation.
- Assumptions: conditions temporarily accepted as true.
- Unknowns: information that is not currently available.

For relevant information, consider recording:

- Source and provenance.
- Date or period to which it applies.
- Reliability and confidence.
- Contradictions or limitations.

Do not present an inference as a verified fact.

### 5. Establish the problem boundaries

Clarify what is actually being addressed.

Distinguish:

- The observed situation.
- Its symptoms.
- Possible underlying causes.
- The problem or opportunity being considered.
- Factors outside the decision's control.

Consider whether relevant factors are:

- Directly controllable.
- Influenced by the decision-maker.
- External and outside direct influence.

Do not assume that the user's initial problem statement identifies
the root cause.

### 6. Identify information gaps

Identify missing information that could materially affect the decision.

Evaluate whether additional investigation is worthwhile.

Possible approaches include:

- Asking stakeholders.
- Consulting documentation.
- Collecting operational data.
- Reviewing historical information.
- Conducting targeted research.
- Testing a hypothesis.

Prioritize investigation according to the potential impact of the
unknown information and the reversibility of the decision.

### 7. Review the context

Update the context when new evidence or circumstances emerge.

Reassess previous interpretations and assumptions when necessary.

Changes in context may require revisiting:

- The domain.
- Objectives.
- Risks and uncertainties.
- Alternatives.
- Expected consequences.

## Interaction guidelines

- Conduct the investigation as a dialogue, not a fixed questionnaire.
- Ask focused questions based on information gaps.
- Avoid asking for information that is already available.
- Distinguish what the user explicitly stated from agent inferences.
- Do not fabricate evidence or fill unknown fields with assumptions.
- Explain why a missing piece of information matters when asking
  the user to provide it.
- Avoid unnecessary investigation when the decision is low-impact
  and easily reversible.
- Revisit previous findings when new information changes the context.

## Expected output

Produce an updated context representation containing, when applicable:

- Current situation.
- Problem or opportunity under consideration.
- Stakeholders and their relevant interests.
- Constraints and dependencies.
- Facts and supporting evidence.
- Hypotheses and assumptions.
- Unknowns and information gaps.
- Relevant external influences.

The output should be structured so it can be incorporated into the
Decision Model.

## Important principles

- Context describes the situation in which a decision exists.
- Context is not necessarily the same as the problem statement.
- Information has different levels of reliability.
- Context is provisional and evolves over time.
- Investigation effort should be proportional to the decision's
  impact, uncertainty, and reversibility.
- New context may change the understanding of the decision itself.
